Dengue IV fluid calculation guide

Adults · CPG Fourth Edition (2026)

MedAssist summary based on the supplied document CPG Dengue4-121026 (2)-1.pdf. This is a quick guide, not an official copy of the CPG. References below use the printed page numbers.

WeightFebrileB2ShockQuotaDiagnosis

Assess first: phase, warning signs, CCTVR, BP/pulse pressure, HCT, oral intake and urine output. The management group alone does not determine the need for IV fluids. Assess the phase clinically; do not infer it from the illness day alone.

Fluid restriction: record the existing regimen under Oral intake, specifying oral or total fluid allowance. The routine non-shock IV and quota calculations below do not establish a safe prescription for that patient; individualise treatment with the treating team. In shock, titrate fluids with frequent perfusion and respiratory reassessment and arrange emergency transfer without delay. CPG pp31,40,48.

1. Weight for calculation

Check weight, height and BMI. If BMI >27.5 kg/m², use adjusted body weight (ABW). The calculator's CPG automatic option uses actual weight for non-obese patients and ABW for obese patients.

Maintenance and quota: calculation weight is capped at 70 kg. This cap does not limit shock resuscitation.

IBW / ABW formulas

Male IBW = 50 + 0.91 × (height in cm − 152) kg.
Female IBW = 45.5 + 0.91 × (height in cm − 152) kg.
ABW = IBW + 0.4 × (actual weight − IBW).

CPG pp40–41; Table 13 and resuscitation principles p48.

2. Febrile phase — maintenance when IV is needed

Encourage oral fluids. IV may be needed if oral fluids are not tolerated or the patient is dehydrated. Total maintenance includes oral + IV, using the 4–2–1 formula:

Weight bandRate
First 10 kg4 mL/kg/hour
Next 10 kg2 mL/kg/hour
Remaining weight1 mL/kg/hour

Example: calculation weight 60 kg
(10 × 4) + (10 × 2) + (40 × 1) = 100 mL/hour oral + IV.
If current oral intake is 40 mL/hour, the IV component = 100 − 40 = IV NS 60 mL/hour.

If oral intake is recorded in mL/24 hours, divide by 24 to obtain the hourly average and confirm that it still reflects current intake. Assess ongoing losses separately. Reduce/stop IV as oral intake improves and dehydration resolves. Moderate/severe dehydration requires hospital management.

CPG p31; Table 11–12 p41; Recommendation 6 p42. Subtracting oral intake is how the calculator estimates the IV component of total maintenance.

3. B2 — warning signs, without shock

B2 requires hospital admission. If oral intake is adequate and there is no dehydration, routine IV may not be needed. CPG p44 states that tolerated oral intake equivalent to 1–2 mL/kg/hour may remove the need for IV; interpret this with the clinical assessment.

If IV is needed because oral fluids are not tolerated: 3–5 mL/kg/hour. Select the initial rate based on assessment, then reassess after 1 hour.

Example: calculation weight 60 kg
3 × 60 = 180 mL/hour
4 × 60 = 240 mL/hour
5 × 60 = 300 mL/hour
Example plan when 3 mL/kg/hour is selected: “IV NS 180 mL/hour. Reassess after 1 hour. Refer ED.”

The text on p44 allows 1–2 hours depending on response; Algorithm 1 uses reassessment after 1 hour. Subsequent rates depend on reassessment and are not an automatic infusion schedule.

CPG Table 6 p28; p44; Algorithm 1 p46.

4. C — with shock

Clinical stateInitial IVExample: calculation weight 60 kg
Compensated shock10 mL/kg over 1 hourIV NS 600 mL over 1 hour
Decompensated shock20 mL/kg over 15–30 minutesIV NS 1200 mL over 15–30 minutes

Reassess CCTVR, BP/pulse pressure, HCT and urine output. Start resuscitation and arrange urgent referral concurrently; do not wait for the infusion to finish before arranging transfer. Subsequent rates/boluses depend on the response and the full algorithm.

Group C also includes severe bleeding and severe organ impairment. Shock is not always present. Do not automatically apply a shock dose or B2 regimen without shock; treatment requires individual assessment in hospital.

NS is 0.9% sodium chloride, one of the crystalloids listed in the CPG. The CPG also lists balanced crystalloids.

CPG Table 6 p28; transfer p30; Algorithms 2–3 pp xv–xvi; crystalloid choices and resuscitation principles pp47–48.

5. Quota — critical phase without shock only

48-hour quota = 1 day's maintenance + 50 mL/kg. This total includes oral and IV intake.

Example: 60 kg
Maintenance for 1 day = 100 × 24 = 2,400 mL.
5% deficit = 50 × 60 = 3,000 mL.
Total = 5,400 mL over 48 hours.

This is not an instruction to use the full quota or divide it into a fixed IV rate. Not every patient needs the full quota. Stop applying this quota if shock develops; reassess when entering the recovery phase.

Printed table versus formula

At 65 kg, the exact formula gives 5,770 mL while the printed table gives 5,700 mL. At 70 kg, the formula gives 6,140 mL while the table gives 6,000 mL. The calculator shows both where applicable; the clinician must confirm the agreed target. No additional rounding rule is assumed.

CPG pp43–46; Appendix 11 p114. The quota supports hospital monitoring and transfer records; it is not an outpatient 48-hour infusion order.

Diagnosis and management groups

Distinguish suspected from confirmed dengue using the assessment and laboratory evidence. A complete diagnosis includes illness day, phase, classification (without warning signs / with warning signs / severe dengue), shock status and comorbidities/special populations.

Confirmed dengue without warning signs, D4, febrile phase, not in shock, NKMI.

A/B1/B2/C guide management decisions and do not need to be added to the diagnosis sentence. Update the diagnosis at each review. Interpret warning signs with the phase and overall clinical condition; vomiting in the febrile phase alone does not automatically classify a patient as B2.

CPG pp14–15; Table 6 p28; Table 7 p30; Recommendation 2 p31.
